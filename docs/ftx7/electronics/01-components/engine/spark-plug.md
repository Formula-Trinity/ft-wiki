## Da Blurb

The spark plug creates the electrical spark that ignites the compressed air-fuel mixture inside the engine cylinder.

Each spark plug is installed in the cylinder head with its electrodes exposed to the combustion chamber.

## Operation

The ignition coil generates a high voltage, typically in the tens of kilovolts.

This voltage is applied across the spark plug electrodes.

When the voltage becomes high enough to overcome the electrical resistance of the gas in the spark plug gap, the gas becomes ionised and an electrical discharge crosses the gap.

This discharge produces the spark used to initiate combustion.

A simplified sequence is:

Ignition Coil → High Voltage → Spark Plug Gap → Spark → Combustion

The actual voltage required to produce a spark varies depending on conditions including:

- spark plug gap;
- cylinder pressure;
- air-fuel mixture;
- temperature;
- spark plug condition.

## Construction

The central conductor of the spark plug carries the high ignition voltage.

The ceramic body surrounding the conductor acts as an electrical insulator. This prevents the high voltage from travelling directly to the cylinder head before reaching the intended spark gap.

The ground electrode is electrically connected to the engine through the threaded body of the spark plug.

The spark therefore crosses between the centre electrode and ground electrode at the end of the plug.

## Spark Plug Gap

The spark plug gap is the distance between the centre and ground electrodes.

The gap must be large enough to produce an effective ignition source while remaining small enough for the ignition system to reliably generate a spark under cylinder pressure.

The required gap for Bruce should be taken from the engine / spark plug specification.



## Inspection & Tests

Spark plugs should be inspected for:

- electrode wear;
- deposits or fouling;
- cracked ceramic;
- correct electrode gap;
- correct tightening;
- signs of abnormal combustion.

Spark plug appearance may also provide useful information about engine operating conditions, although plug reading should be considered alongside ECU and lambda data.
